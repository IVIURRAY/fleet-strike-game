## Description

A **2D** real-time strategy galaxy-conquest autobattler.

Build your galactic empire and crush anyone who stands in your way.

Design, manoeuvre, and captain your starfleet through the void of space. Wield it to wage total war against those who oppose your rule.

Expand your empire across the stars, discovering increasingly complex materials and technologies that can be used to improve your fleet.

Go head-to-head with your friends in multiplayer combat.

- **2D Graphics:** Beautiful sprite-based visuals powered by PixiJS 8 (WebGPU/WebGL)
- Logarithmic scale: manage your galactic empire while also witnessing individual dogfights
- Exciting starship dogfighting
- Economy management
- Deep tactical decision-making

## Inspiration

- **StarCraft** — Space empires fighting for control of the stars, with base-building and unit production.
- **Civilization** — Expand your empire by sending engineers to colonise new planets and harvest their resources for your economy.
- **OpenFront** — Simple, low-fidelity graphics that are satisfying, easy to understand, and straight to the point, while remaining attractive and distinctive.
- **RimWorld** — Simple 2D combat in which each ship has its own AI pathfinder and targeting system. Individual ships should take actions to avoid being hit and use their abilities intelligently. Ships are visually customisable and change as they are upgraded.
- **Direct Strike** — The Warcraft III custom game. Players launch waves of increasingly complex armies designed to counter one another, escalating until the battlefield becomes chaotic.
- **Tower defence games** — Build defensive towers on moons and construct orbital satellite towers.
- **League of Legends** — Use satellites and probes to gather intelligence and discover what your opponent is planning.
- **Team Fortress 2** — Adapt its classic class archetypes into specialised starship roles.

## Economy, Technology Trees, Factions, and Commanders

- Each player has a home planet where they can invest in their economy.
- Players can invest in public schools to produce better-educated pilots, similar to the societal development systems in _Starship Troopers_ or _Civilization_.
- Simulation training can improve pilots’ accuracy and combat performance.
- Manpower must be managed as a strategic resource.
- Players may need to invest in population growth and educate people capable of piloting increasingly complex machines.
- Upgrade the admiral ship’s turret systems.
- Upgrade the admiral ship’s shield systems.
- Upgrade factories to manufacture faster or more capable ships.
- Moons and planets exist throughout the map. Players can conquer them, harvest their unique resources, and use those resources to improve their fleet’s complexity and abilities.
- Manpower is generated by controlling habitable planets and investing in food production.
- Players can send aid packages to planets that are running out of food.
- Stealth ships can infiltrate enemy planets and disrupt their political systems, cutting off manpower production.
- In extreme cases, infiltration can trigger a famine that devastates a planet’s population unless aid packages arrive in time.

## Combat Simulation and Targeting Behaviour

- Ships should use advanced pathfinding to create twisting, unpredictable dogfighting paths.
- Ships should turn, weave, and manoeuvre to avoid incoming fire while attempting to close the distance and gain a tactical advantage.
- Ships being pursued should attempt evasive manoeuvres and look for opportunities to escape or reverse the engagement.
- Robotic systems can perform repairs during or between battles.
- Capturing enemy moons and planets provides additional income and resources.
- Some specialised units should provide crowd-control abilities, including slowing, stunning, hacking, or jamming enemy craft.
- Electronic-warfare ships could disrupt enemy targeting, interfere with satellites, or cause parts of an opposing fleet to make mistakes.

## Galaxy-Control Theme and Progression

- The game takes place across a huge map, potentially requiring logarithmic zooming.
- Players can move seamlessly between a galaxy-wide strategic view and close-up views of individual dogfights.
- Important objects include moons, planets, fleet units, satellites, and each player’s unique admiral ship.
- Territory is primarily contested from left to right, creating a cage-match structure with no map wrapping.
- Alternative map layouts and victory conditions could be introduced later.

## Multiplayer and Network Synchronisation

- Begin with one-versus-one multiplayer using a simple WebSocket connection.
- The simulation should remain deterministic where possible to reduce the amount of state that must be synchronised.
- Players should send commands rather than continuously transmitting the position of every ship.
- The server should remain authoritative over important actions and match outcomes.

## ECS Architecture and PixiJS Rendering

- Use an Entity Component System architecture (bitECS) to layer independent gameplay systems together.
- PixiJS 8 handles 2D rendering with WebGPU/WebGL acceleration while the ECS manages simulation state and behavior.
- Ships, planets, moons, projectiles, satellites, and structures are represented as entities.
- Components contain data, while systems handle behavior such as movement, targeting, combat, production, and rendering.

## TypeScript Domain Models and Data-Driven Ship Definitions

- Define ships, technologies, weapons, factions, and upgrades using data-driven TypeScript models.
- Keep ship statistics separate from their rendering and combat behaviour.
- Allow ship definitions to be loaded from configuration files so that balancing does not require rewriting core systems.
- Upgraded ships should change both statistically and visually.
- Factions should be able to share common systems while retaining unique technologies, ships, and abilities.
